Pulling X posts into transcript·lab

Can we pull an X post, its replies and the author's recent posts cheaply enough to summarise them into the corpus? I ran every route I could find against one real post. Short answer: yes, and for free. FxTwitter's public API returned the full post, all its replies and the author's timeline. Supadata returned the post itself and nothing around it, so it's only worth paying for when an X post has a video to transcribe.

Your questions

Can I pull the post, the comments and the channel?

Each answer comes from a real call against this post on 15 Nov 2026. The raw responses stayed in the session's scratch folder and weren't committed.

Pull this post? YES

Full text, author, date, and views, likes, replies, reposts and bookmarks. FxTwitter returns all of it free. Supadata's /v1/metadata also gives the full text plus basic stats, for 1 credit.

See the comments? YES, FREE

FxTwitter's /2/conversation/:id returned all 3 replies plus the author's 2 follow-ups, with a cursor for more. Supadata metadata only gives the reply count. Its web scrape catches some replies, mixed in with log-in page clutter.

Pull the channel? YES, FREE

FxTwitter's /2/profile/:handle/statuses returned the 21 most recent posts with a paging cursor, and it also returns X Articles in full. X's own syndication timeline returned 429 on both tries. Supadata has no X timeline endpoint.

Is Supadata worth it here? ONLY FOR VIDEO

For text posts it duplicates what the free route gives and adds no replies or timeline. Where it earns its credit is /v1/transcript on an X post that carries a video. It also works as a paid fallback if the free route breaks.

The market · prices checked 15 Nov 2026

What each route costs, and what it covers

Prices come from each provider's own pricing page, linked under each card. On its Basic or Pro plan, Supadata works out at about $0.006–0.017 a credit. That makes even its cheapest call dearer than X's own API, and it covers less.

FxTwitter use now

Free, no key. In our tests it returned full post text, X Articles (9,100 characters), replies, threads and the author's timeline.

  • 1,000 requests/min per IP
  • volunteer-run, no SLA
  • not sanctioned by X
$0 · docs.fxembed.com

Official X API sanctioned

Pay-per-use since Feb 2026; there's no free reading tier any more. Replies come via conversation_id search, timelines via /2/users/:id/tweets.

  • $0.005 a post · $0.010 a user
  • a re-read the same UTC day isn't charged again
  • $20 starter credit
$5 per 1k posts · docs.x.com

twitterapi.io · SocialData unofficial

Paid third-party APIs. Both cover replies, threads, user timelines and Articles. They cost about 1/30 of X's price but have the same terms risk as FxTwitter.

  • twitterapi.io $0.15 per 1k tweets, $1 trial
  • SocialData $0.20 per 1k, no free plan
~$0.2 per 1k · twitterapi.io · socialdata.tools

xAI X Search search, not fetch

Grok picks the calls and answers with citations. x_thread_fetch gets a post with its replies. Good for "what is X saying about…", but too unpredictable for a fixed ingest.

  • $5 per 1k posts + $10 per 1k profiles
  • plus model tokens
$5 per 1k + tokens · docs.x.ai

Supadata video only

Metadata returns the full post text (674 of 674 characters here) but no replies and no timeline. Its real value on X is /v1/transcript, which turns a video post into text.

  • 1 credit for metadata or a native transcript
  • 2 credits a minute when AI-generated
  • credits don't roll over
~$6–17 per 1k posts on Basic/Pro · supadata.ai

Avoid dead or risky

Nitter was archived after X's cease-and-desist (Aug 2026). twscrape needs logged-in X accounts, which risks bans. snscrape hasn't been updated since 2023. X's own oEmbed and syndication endpoints cut long posts off.

Post → one document → summary

A 92-line scratch script (not committed) makes one FxTwitter call, writes the post, the author's thread and the replies as one markdown document, then asks the project's own LLM client (chat_model_kwargs, currently deepseek-v4-flash) for a summary. Nothing was written to the corpus.

Proposal

Each step can be shipped on its own, and nothing spends Supadata credits unless the free route fails or a post has a video.

XClient over FxTwitterThree calls (status, conversation with cursor, profile/statuses with cursor), normalised into one XPost model. Cache responses like discovery_cache, send a descriptive User-Agent, and keep volume low.src/transcripts/x.py · tests with recorded fixtures

For a post with video, fetch /v1/transcript and append it to the body. Risks

What could go wrong

FxTwitter is community-run, not an X product

It proxies X's internal API, comes with no SLA, and can rate-limit, change shape or go dark when X changes something. Keep it behind XClient with a paid fallback, cache aggressively, and never hammer it.

X's terms forbid scraping

X's terms (effective 9 Oct 2026) prohibit crawling or scraping without written consent. FxTwitter and the paid third-party APIs all read X that way. At personal volume the practical risk is low, but only the official API is sanctioned. If this ever powers the public demo, switch XClient to the X API.

Other people's replies

Replies are other people's words. Store their handles only, and keep replies out of the public demo.

Not tested yet

  • Reply paging beyond the first page (this post has 3 replies)
  • Quote posts and polls
  • Supadata /v1/transcript on an X video post
  • FxTwitter in practice under a daily poll (documented limit: 1,000/min)
