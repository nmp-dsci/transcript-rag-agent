02

The cost lens: per-item vs per-minute

the single distinction that decides this budget

Supadata bills two different ways, and the difference is roughly two orders of magnitude. Everything that reads existing text is billed per item. Everything that generates — AI transcription, AI extraction, translation — is billed per minute of media. With 234 credits, per-minute pricing is a rounding error away from spending the lot on one long video.

Per-item — spend freely

234 credits buys a lot of these.

  • 1 credit · one YouTube transcript that already has captions
  • 1 credit · one web page as clean Markdown
  • 1 credit · one whole-site URL map
  • 1 credit · one crawled page
  • 1 credit · a search page of ~20 results

Per-minute — spend deliberately

Budget dies here if you are not counting.

  • 2 / min · AI-generated transcript — a 40-min talk = 80 credits, a third of your balance
  • 5 / min · Extract — a 10-min video = 50 credits for one JSON object
  • 30 / min · Translation — effectively unavailable to you
The non-obvious consequence: short-form video is cheap under per-minute pricing. A 60–90 second TikTok or Reel costs 2–3 credits to transcribe even though no captions exist. Cross-platform expansion is affordable precisely because the clips are short — 15 clips for ~45 credits. Long-form non-YouTube video is not.
Guard rail worth writing into the code: mode currently defaults to "auto" in fetcher.py:53, which silently falls back to AI generation. On YouTube that is usually harmless; pointed at a caption-less 45-minute conference talk it is a 90-credit accident with no warning. Any new ingestion path should take an explicit --max-credits ceiling and refuse to exceed it.

07

Risks worth naming before spending

Credits are not the real cost

Re-indexing 105 new sources triggers your own DeepSeek passes — contextual chunking, graph extraction, themes, conflicts. That bill is larger than 234 Supadata credits and is not covered by this plan.

The demo container has a 1 GB ceiling

App Runner is provisioned at 0.5 vCPU / 1 GB against a measured 539 MiB. Doubling the corpus moves both the image size and the resident set; check before merging, not after the smoke test fails.

Nineteen days, then the balance is gone

Credits reset 11 Sep and do not roll over. Unspent is wasted — but so is data landing in a schema that cannot hold it. The refactor is the gate.

Scraping has terms attached

Supadata's own docs say to respect robots.txt and site terms. For a public portfolio, pick sources whose terms permit it and name them in the write-up — the provenance discipline is part of what the project claims.
