The judge can now see depth

36/36
Scoreboard
Hypothesis: answers that synthesise several creators should outrank faithful restatements of one chunk — the setup ranking should change under a rubric that scores depth, not just grounding.

What shipped in the UI

  • Eight metric columns, each carrying its composite weight in the header (FAITHFUL 20% · PRECISION 10% · RELEVANT 10% · INSIGHT 20% · SPECIFIC 15% · COVERAGE 10%…) — the old three-metric grounding-only composite is gone.
  • The red self-graded banner naming the confound when the answer model also judged.
  • A footnote explaining why one row shows n=19 of 20 rather than silently averaging.
  • Metric explainer popovers (MetricExplainer) for every new depth metric.

What to verify

  • The ranking moved for a checkable reason: rag_agent takes first on insight (0.78 vs 0.46); graph_rag drops to third on evidence breadth 0.20 — it answers off a single subgraph.
  • Weights in the column headers sum to 100%, and the composite matches them.
  • The flip reproduced under an independent OpenAI judge (ρ = τ = +1.00 across all four setups) — harsher, but same order.

V3

Critique quality became a number — then the number was hardened

33/33 · caveat
Experiments →Critique eval panel
Hypothesis: until this panel renders, “better than chunk-dumping” is unfalsifiable. A resume-teardown video is held out of every retrieval path; recall is how many of that expert's criteria the system reached without ever seeing the video they came from.

What shipped in the UI

  • The green held-out absent · 0 leaks chip, the held-out video named.
  • Four metrics with no composite: criteria_recall, evidence_precision, provenance, contested coverage — they trade against each other by design.
  • GATE_PROVENANCE rendering: retrieval arms (rag_llm_filtered, rag_conflict_aware) now show “not measured” over their ungated figure instead of a certified score, and deltas against them are dropped, not subtracted.
  • Per-repeat spread shown on matcher-dependent metrics rather than a bare point estimate.

What to verify

  • The distinct-chunk attack is dead: reciting criteria with one distinct real chunk each previously scored precision 1.000 / recall 0.526 vs the honest 0.364 / 0.158 — now grounds nothing.
  • Every committed run was rescored in place with zero model calls; no score moved, no within-run ranking flipped.
  • Ungraded cells read None, not zero — “not measured” is the honest word.
Open caveat: the relevance question itself. A chunk the finding really did retrieve, cited for a rule it does not support, still passes — only an entailment check (one judged call per citation) reaches it. V5, V6 and V8 are all scored on this metric. Also: the verdict names 3 superseded run files no longer on disk. See src/evals/KNOWN_GAP_attack2.md.
frontend: experiments/CritiquePanel.tsx (+590 lines) · experiments/styles.ts

V7

Disagreements as a first-class view

65/65 · caveat
RAG Pipeline →Disagreements sub-tab
Hypothesis: where creators genuinely disagree should be a browsable layer — each card two positions with their videos and timestamps — and the layer should state its own reliability rather than perform confidence.

What shipped in the UI

  • DisagreementsView (545 lines): conflict cards, each holding both positions with source links, built by index-conflicts and served from GET /api/conflicts.
  • Honesty chrome — the notable part: a warn chip beside the count (“spread not recorded — nearer 2 ± 1 than exactly 2”), card chips in grey reading “agreed 3/3 looks — the most 3 can show”, and a self-agreement chip that no longer calls itself an error bar.
  • The fallback retires automatically on a build that records its own spread — verified against a 9-look artifact on a second instance.

What to verify

  • 2 cards render; the warn chip is present and readable in both themes.
  • Both positions on a card resolve to real videos at the stated timestamps.
  • No chip anywhere overstates: nothing green claims reliability the 3-look measurement can't support.
Open caveat: the count (2) is short of the plan's ≥3 gate — the one soft spot in a 65-check verdict. Reliability is the deeper issue: two builds over an identical candidate pool returned 4 cards then 2, with two cards moving 3/3 → 0/3. Also unaddressed: corpus_fingerprint is computed but never forwarded by list_conflicts, so a reader can't see which corpus the count was measured over.

V8

The deep-research build loop

20/22
Experiments →Deep research panel
Hypothesis: an automated plan → probe → critique loop should build a rubric pack that beats the hand-built merged pack on the held-out critique eval, under the same call budget — and the report should render each round's delta.

What shipped in the UI

  • ResearchPanel (1,002 lines): the build report — plan, per-round probes, a call-budget table (deep-frontier: 1 planner / 10 executor / 1 critic = 12 calls, exactly the hand-built round's), and per-round rubric deltas.
  • The frontier round readout: a coverage table of member videos and how much of each round 1 actually read; round-2 probes barred from re-retrieving round-1 ground (all four probes: 18 chunks each, 0 from round-1 ground).
  • Six arms scored side by side; the evidence_precision badge corrected to “no model in its path” (the earlier wording overclaimed).

What to verify

  • deep-frontier beats merged with disjoint ranges: recall 5/5 repeats above merged's 5/5 (0.4211 floor vs 0.3684 ceiling) — but the margin is one criterion in nineteen; treat it as a narrow win, not a landslide.
  • evidence_precision 0.875 vs 0.824 — the deterministic metric that failed attempt 1 now leads.
  • Old arms' numbers unchanged from attempt 1 — the baseline wasn't moved under the new arm.
The two failed checks — documentation, not UI: the builder's report claimed “recall cannot be bought by dropping rules” (falsified by deep-r2-admit: a strict subset scoring higher, 0.3684 → 0.4211, via the one-to-one matcher freeing criterion c22) and “only deep-frontier reaches c22, via a frontier rule” (it's an inherited round-1 rule, and deep-r2-admit reaches it too). Neither claim appears in the shipped UI — the evaluator searched the rendered page for them and found none — and deep_research.py now documents the correction in its own comments.
frontend: experiments/ResearchPanel.tsx · rag: src/rag/deep_research.py
