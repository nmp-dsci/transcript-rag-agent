A front door for the demo — mirroring Data Pilot's landing flow

Right now a visitor to the live demo lands straight inside the workbench — powerful, but nothing tells them what they're looking at, why it exists, or what the numbers mean. data-qa-agent solves this with a full-screen landing card: brand + tagline, a benefit grid, real example questions, outcome metrics, and one Enter demo → button. This plan ports that flow into transcript·lab's own design system.

The flow, mirrored from Data Pilot

  1. Visitor hits the URL → full-screen landing renders (no app chrome). A demo_landing_view pageview is captured. The corpus fetch starts in the background — Data Pilot's pre-warm trick, reused: by the time they've read the pitch, the app data is loaded and entry feels instant.
  2. They read — what it is (one line), what it does (four benefit cards), what it measures (the metrics strip), and real questions it has answered.
  3. Enter demo → — demo_enter_click captured, a session flag set, the landing unmounts, and Chat opens on the newest judged conversation (existing D0 behaviour). Back-button / #landing returns to the landing; any other hash deep-link (#board, #pipeline) skips it entirely so shared links still land where they point.
No login, unlike Data Pilot: its landing doubles as an auth gate (Google owner sign-in, demo door). Ours has no accounts by design — the landing is purely narrative + door, which makes it simpler: one component, one flag, no auth wiring.
